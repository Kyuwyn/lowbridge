ADR 0031: The basin road's system expansions: hobby slot, Focus, foe techniques, lasting preparations (stances withdrawn)

Status: Accepted semantics for the engine expansions shipped with the basin road: a hobby slot, a second strain gauge named Focus, foe techniques, and preparations that last. Fight stances were accepted the same day and **withdrawn the same evening** after the twelve-skill design audit (see §3). Every rate, length and bonus is content and provisional. Date: 27 October 2026. Basis: the owner's decision of 27 October that every system grows with the next chapter; [ADR 0022](adr-0022-chore-tempo.md) (one slot, a waiting list, `autoStart` Rest), [ADR 0026](adr-0026-combat-exchanges.md) (fights, `held`, the exact win chance), [ADR 0029](adr-0029-crafting-workbench-recipes-preparations.md) (recipes, doses, `preparation`), [ADR 0030](adr-0030-sphere-techniques.md), design plan Part A rules 12 and 13, and rules 5, 13, 20, 23, 24, 28 and 29 of the design rules ([`research/arcanum-reference/12-design-rules.md`](../../research/arcanum-reference/12-design-rules.md)).

**Context.** The ridge chapter grew content on an engine that did not grow: one slot, one strain gauge, fights decided by held methods alone, preparations that pay once on the click. The design plan promised each of the four as a later reward (rule 12's "strain gauges", rule 13's "B3 grants one hobby slot", ADR 0026's open items "stances", "foe techniques" and "per-swing effects", ADR 0029's open "whether a preparation should last"). The basin road is where they land, and each is sized to the reference rule that names it:

- Rule 23 and 24: **one slot + auto-filling rest + `fill`**; **slots are rare named milestones**, and **priority flags (Goal/Hobby) + a bounded waiting list are the automation**, *copy*. Rule 13 (design plan): the hobby is a zero-attention filler.
- Rule 29: **noncombat sites use five stresses as HP**, *copy*.
- Rule 28: **auto-battler turn = one attack + one spell per speed tick; quickslots are the active layer**, *copy*.
- Rule 5 and 20: **resources carry mods**; **passive conversion is owned furniture**, *copy*.

**Decision.**

1. **A hobby slot.** A record marked `hobby: true` (only with `length: "continuous"`, never `autoStart`) may run in a second place beside the activity slot, at **half pace** (`HOBBY_RATE = 0.5`): its `run` and `strain` are paid in full each second, its `effect` and the family it trains land at half, its `ticks` and `at` thresholds count whole seconds. The hobby is chosen, not scheduled: it has no objective, is never accelerated by the bank, and the waiting list ignores it. Starting the same record in the main slot empties the hobby; a hobby that cannot sustain itself, fills its `fill` store, or loses its availability stops with the main slot's wording; a lock on its record clears it with the "this chapter has closed" line. It runs while away, since away time already drives the tick. The slot is a counted store, `hobby_slots` ("Hobby"), whose `value` is 1 while a hobby runs and whose `max` content raises: the Second Direction grants the first. Marked in content: the slip's breath practice and copying, the four sphere drills, and the refining drills; never Rest, steady attention or the channel exercise, so the opening's pace is untouched.

2. **Focus, a second gauge.** `focus` is a shared resource revealed at the waystation (8/8, +0.02/s; Rest refills it, as it refills Vigor and Life; Strain is not Rest's to refill). Basin outings drain Strain and Focus in different proportions: a loaded climb is mostly Strain, a relay test mostly Focus, so a player holding Earth or Formation methods that raise Focus takes the attentive outings more easily and a Body player the heavy ones. No engine change is needed: a `strain` table already drains any named store and an unpayable second refuses or pauses the run (ADR 0022); the Body panel lists Focus after Strain. The deep earth halves, the formation half that wards the threshold, and the Second Direction raise its cap; the settling draught discounts it. The design audit of 27 October found formation's own site (the relay stones) sat at zero margin against the cap, so its drain is 0.04 like the other attentive sites and the ward raises the cap by two.

3. **Stances: withdrawn.** The first cut gave a technique marked `stance: true` a `held` table and nothing else, one taken at a time, free to swap, folded into the fight stats like an attuned method, with the flip hint naming the stance that would turn a fight; three arrived at the waystation (Rooted, Open, Pressing). The design audit of the same day found them working against the chapter's thesis (ADR 0030 §3, identity is what is held at a price): a stance paid for free, in no place and under no lock, the same +2 Guard a deep half charges five qi, a place and a permanent lock for; the chapter's one fight was their only consumer; the flip hint solved the choice; and they were the first "technique" the basin introduced, before the player had met a deep pair. The owner removed them. The `stance` field, the Stances group, the save field and the grammar are gone; `holding()` reads attuned methods and preparations in effect. The spur pair keeps its rush of sixteen: the held Guard method now does what the stance did, and the flip hint names the method and what holding it closes. Foe techniques stay (§4). Rule 28's divergence below is therefore moot for this slice; a pre-fight loadout, if it returns, should cost what a method costs.

4. **Foe techniques.** A foe may carry `technique: { name, every, strike }`: its *k*-th swing, counted from one across the whole fight, uses `technique.strike` instead of `strike` when `k` is a multiple of `every`, and the fight log names it ("the two at the spur: the rush.") before the ordinary landed-or-slipped line. The exact win chance of ADR 0026 §5 already replays the fixed swing timeline second by second over every outcome; it now reads the foe's strike per swing from the swing count, so the chance stays exact, and it also yields the mass-weighted mean time of each outcome, which replaces the average-race figures the tooltip showed as "time to fell". The verdict bands, the *hopeless* rule (no swing of yours can deal damage) and the flip order are unchanged. The first foe with a technique is the pair at the spur, sized so that a held Guard method is what flips it at real waystation stats (life 18, strike 10, guard 6, speed 1.2, hit 80, dodge 20, the rush every third swing at strike 16): on the body line at the waystation (Guard 8) it reads a likely loss at about 41%, with *Temper the marrow* held (Guard 10) a certain win at about 98%, and on the sword line (Guard 5) a likely loss at about 48%.

5. **Lasting preparations.** A `preparation` may carry `lasts` (activity seconds) and, only then, a `held` table. Taking it spends its dose and pays its `result` on the click as before, and also puts `{ id, remaining: lasts }` into a saved `effects` list, replacing an entry with the same id, so drinking again resets the clock and nothing stacks. Every activity second (the main slot's or a hobby-only second, once) decrements `remaining`; at zero the entry drops with a "wears off" line. While in effect its `held` is paid exactly as an attuned method's. Effects run down while away, and through Rest. The settling draught lasts ten minutes (it discounts Strain and Focus drains by a quarter, and the Rest that follows a drained outing would otherwise eat half of it); the marrow draught lasts five (it speeds the carrying drills and the scree climb, which run while it counts). The clear pill pays at once and does not last.

6. **One contribution rule.** The engine's one place where standing contributions join (`eachContribution`) now visits a record's `held` when it is attuned **or** it is a preparation in effect. Nothing else in the contribution machinery changes, so `recompute`, tooltips and the fight capture read both the same way.

7. **Save v7.** `hobby` (an active task or null, decoded as `active` is) and `effects` (entries refused unless the id is a `preparation` and `remaining` is a finite positive number) join the authoritative state. A v6 file is refused with the existing version wording, not migrated (ADR 0025's practice). (`stance` was in the first v7 cut and was removed with §3 before any save shipped; a file carrying the key is read as if it did not.)

**Divergences from *copy* rules, with reasons.**

- **Rule 29 (five stresses) is copied with two gauges, not five.** Strain and Focus are the two; the spheres differ in which they draw on, which is what five stresses are for in the reference. Reason: the reference's five map onto its five knowledge currencies; Lowbridge's outings pay families, not currencies, and two gauges that different methods restore is the smallest number that gives a sphere a preference.
- **Rule 28 (quickslots as the active layer): not taken up.** A fight here is one timed run that cannot be left or steered, so an active layer would be a click with nothing to decide after the first second. The stance that stood in for it was withdrawn (§3); what you hold before entry is the whole loadout.
- **Rule 24 (Goal and Hobby as priority flags) is copied as a named slot resource.** The hobby is a second place rather than a flag on the queue. Reason: a flag on a six-deep waiting list would make the hobby an order of service, and the plan's rule 13 asked for a filler that runs *beside* the slot while the player is away.
- **Rule 5 and 20 are copied.** A lasting preparation is a held thing carrying a mod for a time; the kit of ADR 0029 remains the permanent form.

**Consequences.** The interface gains a Hobby line under the current activity with a Stop button, a Hobby button on continuous cards once the slot exists, an in-effect block in the Craft tab's Preparations section with time left, "lasts N min" in the tooltip's `time` block, the technique line on the fight pane's foe side, and Focus in the Body panel. The validator admits `hobby` and `lasts` with their grammar, checks a foe technique's `every` and `strike`, and witnesses the hobby, a lasting draught and the spur fight on its routes. Tests cover the hobby's half pace and stops, the foe technique's damage and the DP against seeded rolls, a lasting preparation's window and reset, and the v7 round-trip with the refusals. The opening's and the ridge's bands are unchanged: nothing here is reachable before the waystation except the `hobby` marks, which do nothing until a slot exists.

**Supersessions.** Supersedes design plan Part A rule 12's "strain gauges" wording with the two named gauges, Strain and Focus; supersedes rule 13's hobby schedule (B3 grants it) with "the Second Direction grants one hobby slot". Settles ADR 0026's open foe techniques, and ADR 0029's open lasting preparation; ADR 0026's open "stances" is settled as *not for this slice* (§3). Amends ADR 0026 §5: the "time to fell" figures are mean outcome times from the exact replay, not average races.

Still open: whether a hobby should ever run at full pace (a later arrangement could raise `HOBBY_RATE` through content if it became a derived field); a third gauge; per-swing effects beyond a heavier strike (healing, Qi spent in a fight); whether a pre-fight loadout should return at a method's price once the basin has more than one fight; whether an effect should stack to a cap rather than reset; and the balance of every figure above.
