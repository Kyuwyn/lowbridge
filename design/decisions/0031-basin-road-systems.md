ADR 0031: The basin road's four system expansions: hobby slot, Focus, stances and foe techniques, lasting preparations

Status: Accepted semantics for four engine expansions shipped with the basin road: a hobby slot, a second strain gauge named Focus, fight stances and foe techniques, and preparations that last. Every rate, length and bonus is content and provisional. Date: 27 October 2026. Basis: the owner's decision of 27 October that every system grows with the next chapter; [ADR 0022](0022-chore-tempo.md) (one slot, a waiting list, `autoStart` Rest), [ADR 0026](0026-combat-exchanges.md) (fights, `held`, the exact win chance), [ADR 0029](0029-crafting-workbench-recipes-preparations.md) (recipes, doses, `preparation`), [ADR 0030](0030-sphere-techniques.md), design plan Part A rules 12 and 13, and rules 5, 13, 20, 23, 24, 28 and 29 of the design rules ([`research/arcanum-reference/12-design-rules.md`](../../research/arcanum-reference/12-design-rules.md)).

**Context.** The ridge chapter grew content on an engine that did not grow: one slot, one strain gauge, fights decided by held methods alone, preparations that pay once on the click. The design plan promised each of the four as a later reward (rule 12's "strain gauges", rule 13's "B3 grants one hobby slot", ADR 0026's open items "stances", "foe techniques" and "per-swing effects", ADR 0029's open "whether a preparation should last"). The basin road is where they land, and each is sized to the reference rule that names it:

- Rule 23 and 24: **one slot + auto-filling rest + `fill`**; **slots are rare named milestones**, and **priority flags (Goal/Hobby) + a bounded waiting list are the automation**, *copy*. Rule 13 (design plan): the hobby is a zero-attention filler.
- Rule 29: **noncombat sites use five stresses as HP**, *copy*.
- Rule 28: **auto-battler turn = one attack + one spell per speed tick; quickslots are the active layer**, *copy*.
- Rule 5 and 20: **resources carry mods**; **passive conversion is owned furniture**, *copy*.

**Decision.**

1. **A hobby slot.** A record marked `hobby: true` (only with `length: "continuous"`, never `autoStart`) may run in a second place beside the activity slot, at **half pace** (`HOBBY_RATE = 0.5`): its `run` and `strain` are paid in full each second, its `effect` and the family it trains land at half, its `ticks` and `at` thresholds count whole seconds. The hobby is chosen, not scheduled: it has no objective, is never accelerated by the bank, and the waiting list ignores it. Starting the same record in the main slot empties the hobby; a hobby that cannot sustain itself, fills its `fill` store, or loses its availability stops with the main slot's wording; a lock on its record clears it with the "this chapter has closed" line. It runs while away, since away time already drives the tick. The slot is a counted store, `hobby_slots` ("Hobby"), whose `value` is 1 while a hobby runs and whose `max` content raises: the Second Direction grants the first. Marked in content: the slip's breath practice and copying, the four sphere drills, and the refining drills; never Rest, steady attention or the channel exercise, so the opening's pace is untouched.

2. **Focus, a second gauge.** `focus` is a shared resource revealed at the waystation (8/8, +0.02/s; Rest refills it, as it refills Strain and Life). Basin outings drain Strain and Focus in different proportions: a loaded climb is mostly Strain, a relay test mostly Focus, so a player holding Earth or Formation methods that raise Focus takes the attentive outings more easily and a Body player the heavy ones. No engine change is needed: a `strain` table already drains any named store and an unpayable second refuses or pauses the run (ADR 0022); the Body panel lists Focus after Strain. The deep earth and formation halves and the Second Direction raise its cap; the settling draught discounts it.

3. **Stances, a second exclusive loadout.** A technique marked `stance: true` carries a `held` table and nothing else that a method carries (no `attune`, `cost`, `length` or `result`). At most one stance is taken at a time; taking another replaces it, and dropping it costs nothing. A stance's `held` is paid like an attuned method's, so the fight stats fold it in with no fight-specific code, and the verdict's flip hint gains a candidate, "taking the Rooted stance: about 84%", evaluated by re-projecting with that stance's table. Three stances arrive with the waystation, each a trade: Rooted (Guard +2, Speed −0.2), Open (Dodge +10, Guard −1), Pressing (Strike +2, Dodge −5). A stance is saved as the id taken.

4. **Foe techniques.** A foe may carry `technique: { name, every, strike }`: its *k*-th swing, counted from one across the whole fight, uses `technique.strike` instead of `strike` when `k` is a multiple of `every`, and the fight log names it ("the two at the spur: the rush.") before the ordinary landed-or-slipped line. The exact win chance of ADR 0026 §5 already replays the fixed swing timeline second by second over every outcome; it now reads the foe's strike per swing from the swing count, so the chance stays exact, and it also yields the mass-weighted mean time of each outcome, which replaces the average-race figures the tooltip showed as "time to fell". The verdict bands, the *hopeless* rule (no swing of yours can deal damage) and the flip order are unchanged. The first foe with a technique is the pair at the spur (life 14, strike 7, guard 4, speed 1.2, hit 80, dodge 20, the rush every third swing at strike 12): at Foundation stats it reads Cannot win; with Guard 6 (the Body line and the Rooted stance) the rush does 6 and the fight is likely.

5. **Lasting preparations.** A `preparation` may carry `lasts` (activity seconds) and, only then, a `held` table. Taking it spends its dose and pays its `result` on the click as before, and also puts `{ id, remaining: lasts }` into a saved `effects` list, replacing an entry with the same id, so drinking again resets the clock and nothing stacks. Every activity second (the main slot's or a hobby-only second, once) decrements `remaining`; at zero the entry drops with a "wears off" line. While in effect its `held` is paid exactly as a stance's or an attuned method's. Effects run down while away. Two draughts last five minutes: the settling draught discounts Strain and Focus drains by a quarter, the marrow draught speeds the carrying drills and the scree climb. The clear pill pays at once and does not last.

6. **One contribution rule.** The engine's one place where standing contributions join (`eachContribution`) now visits a record's `held` when it is attuned, **or** it is the stance taken, **or** it is a preparation in effect. Nothing else in the contribution machinery changes, so `recompute`, tooltips and the fight capture read all three the same way.

7. **Save v7.** `hobby` (an active task or null, decoded as `active` is), `effects` (entries refused unless the id is a `preparation` and `remaining` is a finite positive number) and `stance` (refused unless a technique with `stance: true`) join the authoritative state. A v6 file is refused with the existing version wording, not migrated (ADR 0025's practice).

**Divergences from *copy* rules, with reasons.**

- **Rule 29 (five stresses) is copied with two gauges, not five.** Strain and Focus are the two; the spheres differ in which they draw on, which is what five stresses are for in the reference. Reason: the reference's five map onto its five knowledge currencies; Lowbridge's outings pay families, not currencies, and two gauges that different methods restore is the smallest number that gives a sphere a preference.
- **Rule 28 (quickslots as the active layer): diverged.** A stance is held, not clicked: it is set before the fight and read at entry with the rest of the stats (ADR 0026 §3). Reason: a fight here is one timed run that cannot be left or steered, so an active layer would be a click with nothing to decide after the first second; the choice of stance before entry is the decision, and the flip hint names it.
- **Rule 24 (Goal and Hobby as priority flags) is copied as a named slot resource.** The hobby is a second place rather than a flag on the queue. Reason: a flag on a six-deep waiting list would make the hobby an order of service, and the plan's rule 13 asked for a filler that runs *beside* the slot while the player is away.
- **Rule 5 and 20 are copied.** A lasting preparation is a held thing carrying a mod for a time; the kit of ADR 0029 remains the permanent form.

**Consequences.** The interface gains a Hobby line under the current activity with a Stop button, a Hobby button on continuous cards once the slot exists, a Stances group on the Techniques tab with Take/Taken, "· Stance: X" on the Techniques header, an in-effect block in the Craft tab's Preparations section with time left, "lasts 5 min" in the tooltip's `time` block, the technique line on the fight pane's foe side, and Focus in the Body panel. The validator admits `hobby`, `stance` and `lasts` with their grammar, checks a foe technique's `every` and `strike`, and witnesses the hobby, every stance, a lasting draught and the spur fight on its routes. Tests cover the hobby's half pace and stops, stance replacement and capture, the foe technique's damage and the DP against seeded rolls, a lasting preparation's window and reset, and the v7 round-trip with the refusals. The opening's and the ridge's bands are unchanged: nothing here is reachable before the waystation except the `hobby` marks, which do nothing until a slot exists.

**Supersessions.** Supersedes design plan Part A rule 12's "strain gauges" wording with the two named gauges, Strain and Focus; supersedes rule 13's hobby schedule (B3 grants it) with "the Second Direction grants one hobby slot". Settles ADR 0026's open stances and foe techniques, and ADR 0029's open lasting preparation. Amends ADR 0026 §5: the "time to fell" figures are mean outcome times from the exact replay, not average races.

Still open: whether a hobby should ever run at full pace (a later arrangement could raise `HOBBY_RATE` through content if it became a derived field); a third gauge; per-swing effects beyond a heavier strike (healing, Qi spent in a fight); whether stances should cost qi to hold; whether an effect should stack to a cap rather than reset; and the balance of every figure above.
