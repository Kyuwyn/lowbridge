Residence and home development: working design

Status: Fourth design checkpoint. Broad world, residence, capacity, and crafting scope accepted, 16 October 2026. [ADR 0006](../docs/architecture/adr-0006-world-and-residence.md) records a few connected regions, one current residence, combined cultivation and development benefits, reusable investments carrying across moves, choices within home capacity, and focused crafting. The examples below are proposals; exact residence and improvement rosters, capacity units and values, reconfiguration rules, recipes, and costs remain open.

A home should help the player establish a foothold, pursue cultivation, and prepare for the next undertaking. Choosing a residence combines two considerations: what its conditions make possible, and how far the player can develop its facilities. Home improvements support the cultivator's one primary activity. The village remains the base immediately after the first foundation.

**Illustrative residences**

| Example, not a selected roster | Cultivation possibilities | Development potential |
|---|---|---|
| Village dwelling | Familiar surroundings support early practice and preparations for nearby investigations. | Modest improvements establish independence; the opening already includes an optional practice upgrade and the channel's useful arrangement. |
| Spacious courtyard residence | Open ground could support demanding exercises and preparations requiring room. | More room could accommodate substantial facilities and a wider choice of improvements. |
| Retreat near a steady qi source | Its conditions could support delicate or sustained practice that is difficult elsewhere. | Developing the site could make better use of its natural advantage, while usable room may remain more constrained. |

These examples illustrate the accepted combination. They do not select specific effects, path restrictions, mandatory moves, or a sequence of residence tiers. Each path needs useful home choices; basic arrangements remain shared rather than belonging exclusively to Formation.

**Reusable investments and work at the new site**

Reusable equipment and knowledge come with the cultivator. Arrangements tied to the destination take work to establish. For example, practice implements could travel, while preparing a courtyard or adapting an arrangement to a new qi source could require fresh effort. These classifications and exact costs are proposed examples of the accepted rule.

The treatment of structures left behind, permission to return to an old residence, storage, and any reuse of former arrangements remain open. The campaign still centers home development on one current residence. Checkpoint retries restore the home state captured by the checkpoint exactly.

**Accepted home capacity and crafting scope**

Useful improvements compete for what the residence can support at once. The player chooses a setup suited to current goals; expanding the home lets it support more. This sits alongside distinctive natural conditions and greater development potential. Capacity units and values, furniture slots, reconfiguration rules, and any placement interface remain undecided.

Design recommendation: a setup should support a sustained objective or stretch of play. Its benefits should be clear enough to choose deliberately, without making frequent rearrangement necessary for ordinary progress. This is guidance for developing the accepted capacity rule, not a selected switching cost or restriction.

Crafting is focused preparation: make selected equipment and arrangements for exploration, home development, and trials. [ADR 0009](../docs/architecture/adr-0009-work-and-crafting-resources.md) establishes currency and selected materials as its resource model; some preparations require materials with meaningful sources and uses. Paid work remains useful throughout the campaign alongside expedition rewards. Concrete jobs, recipes, materials, and acquisition routes remain open. Purchases, discoveries, construction, or help from others are possible sources to design; broad crafting professions and production chains have not been selected.

Exact residence conditions, expansion limits, improvement effects, reconfiguration rules, recipes, relocation requirements, setup costs, and travel costs need later design. Pacing must account for preparation competing with cultivation and exploration for the same activity time and lifespan.
