## Three quantities to keep separate

| Quantity | Meaning for this design |
|---|---|
| Real elapsed time | Time since the player started, including gaps between visits. A calendar target depends on when the player returns to make decisions. |
| Simulated activity time | Work actually carried out by the cultivator, including activity processed while away or accelerated with banked time. One primary activity runs, stopping at its objective or a meaningful decision. |
| Age and remaining lifespan | The consequence of simulated activity. Pauses and storing time do not age the cultivator; accelerated work still has its normal lifespan cost. Exact age conversion and extensions remain open. |

[ADR 0007](decisions/0007-away-time-and-acceleration.md) governs the bank: unused time accrues while open or closed, to a fixed cap intended to cover a few days. Time already used for activity is not credited again. Acceleration is enabled for the current objective and ends at its completion or a meaningful pause. Q120 fixes one-for-one exchange and Q121 prompt delivery below. Exact bank capacity and presentation remain undecided; activity-to-fictional-time conversion remains separate.

[ADR 0002](decisions/0002-lifespan-and-retries.md) governs lifespan. Ordinary setbacks retain established knowledge and progress while spending elapsed activity and used preparation. Lifespan exhaustion instead restores the previous breakthrough's character and world state, or the opening state before foundation. The bank keeps its current balance across that restore. The withdrawal in the example below is an ordinary setback, not a checkpoint replay.

## Accepted common activity time scale — Q119

**Q119 accepts one shared scale from simulated activity time to fictional elapsed time throughout the opening through breakthrough 4.** Work, travel, study, practice, crafting, and trial execution that consume equal normal activity time advance the fiction and the cultivator's age by the same amount. The exact conversion is still to be derived from the workload and Q117's target.

The ordinary activity time actually needed to complete an objective determines its fictional duration. An efficiency benefit that reduces that required time also saves lifespan. A benefit that changes yield, quality, or access without shortening duration need not save lifespan. Banked acceleration executes the same amount of activity faster for the player and preserves its ordinary fictional time and age cost. Paused decisions and unspent bank advance neither activity nor age.

Longer objectives must be written as sustained work, travel, study, or preparation that fits their fictional duration. An isolated trivial errand should not inexplicably consume months just because its progress bar is long.

This decision settles the conversion rule for the early campaign only. It does not assign a rate, individual action durations, later-campaign time scales, lifespan extensions, or NPC aging behavior. Those remain separate design work; choosing a common scale is not evidence that the current routes meet all timing and reserve targets.

## Accepted bank exchange — Q120

**Q120 accepts conserving normal activity time one for one.** One unused real hour adds one bank hour, subject to the agreed cap. Spending one bank hour supplies one additional hour of normal activity progress. Ordinary elapsed activity and spent bank are accounted for separately; an elapsed portion cannot both advance work and become a new bank credit.

Existing boundaries remain: acceleration applies only to the chosen current objective, ends at a meaningful pause or completion, and preserves the age cost of the work actually completed. Remaining bank stays available. The fixed cap is still to be selected. This settles the exchange ratio; Q121 settles delivery during a visit below.

The bank preserves unused opportunities to progress; the accepted exchange adds no progress bonus for deliberately waiting.

## Accepted spending experience — Q121

**Q121 accepts applying available stored time promptly to the current objective when the player enables its use.** Spend only enough to finish that objective or reach its next meaningful pause, preserving any unused bank. If the balance is insufficient, advance as far as it supports and let remaining work continue at ordinary speed. The player must explicitly enable spending again for another objective or continuation after a meaningful pause.

This lets a short visit include several decisions when enough time has accumulated, while retaining the accepted one-activity and decision-pause rules. It does not choose a subsequent activity, bypass prerequisites, or reduce the fictional time of completed work. Exact controls, brief presentation feedback, and processing implementation remain open; bank delivery introduces no substantial extra wait.

A fixed fast-forward multiplier is therefore unnecessary for this delivery model. The remaining calendar checks concern activity duration, decision timing, short-visit capacity, and storage losses; prompt spending does not itself prove the accepted calendar targets.

## Conservation check after Q120–Q121

For an uninterrupted fresh attempt, before any checkpoint restore, the accepted exchange implies:

**Real elapsed time = activity time completed + current bank balance + time discarded at the cap.**

Use consistent normal-time units. Decision pauses contribute to the bank, not to extra activity. This identity includes time processed while away and current partial activity; it assumes no preloaded bank or external grants. A checkpoint retry needs a separate account of activity subsequently erased from character state, because spent bank is not refunded. The curious example's ordinary withdrawal does not erase its elapsed activity, so the identity still applies to that route.

Prompt delivery lets the player spend time accumulated during a pause after choosing what to do next. With enough room in a short visit for the available choices, additional pauses therefore need not create a lasting calendar delay. They can still affect the current balance and the next opportunity to decide. Reading and decision time, limited visit length, and storage losses need explicit modeling rather than being hidden in a route total.

The following arithmetic is a sensitivity check, **not an allocated curious workload or a playthrough simulation**. It assumes no cap losses. The last two columns simply round the work threshold up to the next evenly spaced visit, assuming the player can make all supported choices there; they do not prove that a real short visit accommodates them. Actual completion may happen between visits where the activity permits it.

## Accepted rhythm between decisions — Q122

**Q122 accepts that the main journey normally reach a meaningful next decision within roughly 8–12 hours of ordinary activity, with longer commitments as explicitly designed exceptions.** That corresponds to the gap between the reference player's two or three short daily visits. It is a target for designing the sequence of existing activities, not a guarantee for every schedule, a timer that interrupts work, or a requirement to add a choice to every small action. A visit can include several brief observations, purchases, and choices before starting a substantial objective.

Occasional longer commitments can be designed explicitly, but a single cultivation objective occupying several consecutive visits is not the normal early-campaign pattern. Banked time can bring decisions closer together in a visit; the ordinary-speed target must still make sense when the bank is empty.

The provisional 176-hour envelope must be checked against this selected rhythm; it does not override it.

The [worked budget](PACING-PAPER-BUDGET.md) allocates the accepted teaching, investigation, practice, livelihood, travel, and trial work, revising the unaccepted workload envelope and testing a different candidate age conversion. Existing optional projects can provide variety, but they do not prove that the focused route offers the intended decisions. Do not subdivide a practice bar into compulsory examinations or arbitrary confirmation clicks to manufacture decision frequency. Q123 resolves its calendar direction below.

## Decisions that follow the reference pace

1. Refine Q123's accepted compact direction using the first worked budget. Refine the candidate workloads, representative reserve activities, and interaction assumptions; include actual prices and any supply shortfalls when those exist.
2. Review the candidate conversion against Q117's accepted span, Q118's starting values, and Q119's common scale, including the fictional duration of each activity. Then set breakthrough extensions and address how recurring people and places reflect those years.
3. Preserve Q122's accepted decision rhythm and Q120–Q121's bank behavior while refining the schedule. Check short and sustained objectives, visit capacity, and storage losses without manufacturing delays to force a calendar target.
